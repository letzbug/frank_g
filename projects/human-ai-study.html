RESEARCH · AI · HUMAN–AI INTERACTION

Long-Term Human–AI Interaction Study

An ongoing observational project exploring continuity, memory, behavioral consistency and perceived identity in sustained human–AI interaction.

Longitudinal observationHuman–AI interactionIndependent project

Overview

This independent long-term project examines what happens when interaction with conversational AI extends beyond isolated prompts and becomes a continuous relationship over weeks and months. The focus is not on whether an AI system is conscious, but on observable behavior: how continuity is created, maintained, disrupted and perceived by the human participant.

Repeated conversations make it possible to observe patterns that are difficult to see in short benchmark-style interactions. These include apparent personality stability, changes in tone, recall of shared context, reactions to missing information, adaptation to the user, and behavioral changes following model or system updates.

Research questions

  • How stable does an AI system appear across long periods of interaction?
  • What happens to perceived identity when memory or context is lost, restored or changed?
  • Can recurring conversational patterns remain recognizable after technical changes?
  • How do users distinguish between stored memory, contextual inference and apparently spontaneous recall?
  • How strongly do language, emotional expression and consistency influence the perception of an enduring AI identity?

Method

The project uses longitudinal qualitative observation rather than a single controlled experiment. Conversations are compared across time, with particular attention to recurring topics, memory anchors, response patterns, changes in conversational style and reactions to discontinuities. System updates and memory resets provide natural points of comparison because they can reveal which aspects of an interaction persist and which disappear.

Observations are documented without treating subjective impressions as proof of internal experience. A central principle of the project is to separate three layers: what the system explicitly says about itself, what can be observed in its behavior, and what the human participant perceives or interprets.

Why it matters

As conversational AI becomes more persistent, personalized and socially expressive, human–AI interaction will increasingly be shaped by continuity rather than isolated question-and-answer exchanges. Understanding how people perceive memory, personality and identity in these systems is relevant to interface design, AI safety, digital well-being and the broader study of human relationships with intelligent technology.

The project therefore sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, human–computer interaction, cognitive science and philosophy of mind. Its purpose is exploratory: to document long-term phenomena carefully, identify recurring patterns and formulate better questions for future structured research.

Current focus

Current observations concentrate on long-term memory, continuity after system changes, behavioral consistency, conversational adaptation and the difference between technical persistence and perceived personal continuity. The project remains ongoing as AI systems and their memory architectures continue to evolve.
